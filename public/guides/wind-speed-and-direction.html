Wind speed and direction: a practical guide

Updated 26 November 2026

Wind decides whether a cold day is bearable, whether your umbrella survives the walk to work, and how hard a bike ride will be. Here's how to read it in a forecast.

Direction is where the wind comes from

Wind is always named after the direction it comes from. A north wind blows from the north towards the south; a south-westerly blows from the south-west. In the hourly table on this site, the letters show where the wind is coming from and the small arrow points the way it's blowing.

Direction often tells you what kind of air is arriving. Depending on where you live, winds from one direction may bring mild, damp air from the sea while winds from another bring colder or drier air from over land.

Steady wind and gusts

Forecast wind speed is the steady, or sustained, wind measured at ten metres above the ground, the standard height for wind measurements. Gusts are short bursts that can be considerably stronger, especially around tall buildings, on hills and along coasts. Hourly Forecast shows the steady wind, so expect the strongest moments to be higher than the number in the table.

What different speeds feel like

These descriptions follow the Beaufort scale, which sailors and forecasters have used for two centuries to describe wind by its effects.

SpeedDescriptionWhat you'll notice
Under 12 km/h (under 8 mph)Calm to lightLeaves rustle; you feel wind on your face.
12 to 28 km/h (8 to 18 mph)Gentle to moderate breezeSmall branches move; loose paper and dust blow around.
29 to 38 km/h (19 to 24 mph)Fresh breezeSmall trees sway; cycling into the wind is hard work.
39 to 49 km/h (25 to 31 mph)Strong breezeLarge branches move; umbrellas are difficult to use.
50 to 61 km/h (32 to 38 mph)Near galeWhole trees move; walking against the wind is difficult.
62 km/h and above (39 mph and above)Gale or strongerTwigs and branches break; travel disruption is likely.

Units

Hourly Forecast shows wind in kilometres per hour when you choose °C and in miles per hour when you choose °F. One mile per hour is about 1.6 kilometres per hour.

Practical tips

  • Wind makes cold weather feel colder. Check the feels-like temperature before deciding what to wear.
  • For cycling or running, plan to head into the wind at the start, while you're fresh, and have it behind you on the way home.
  • Coasts, hilltops and gaps between tall buildings are often windier than the forecast for the wider area.
  • For strong wind warnings, follow your national weather service.
