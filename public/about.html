About Hourly Forecast

Hourly Forecast answers the questions you have before heading out: will it rain, when, and how warm will it be? It shows the weather hour by hour for the next 48 hours, for any city in the world.

What you'll find on the forecast page

  • The outlook. One sentence at the top tells you when rain or snow is likely in the next 24 hours, or that it should stay dry.
  • The next 48 hours. A strip you can scroll sideways, with the temperature line, the chance of rain or snow, and where sunset and sunrise fall.
  • Hour by hour. A table with temperature, feels-like temperature, chance of rain or snow, wind, humidity and UV index for each hour.
  • Next 7 days. Each day's low and high, shown against the whole week's range so warm and cold days stand out.

How to read the 48-hour strip

Each column is one hour, tinted by what the sky is doing:

  • Clear or mostly clear daytime sky
  • Cloud or fog
  • Rain, drizzle or thunderstorms
  • Night-time hours
  • Sunrise or sunset

The orange line traces the temperature. The blue bars along the bottom show the chance of rain or snow in that hour: the taller the bar, the more likely it is.

Where the data comes from

Forecasts come from Open-Meteo, which combines weather models from national weather services. They update every hour, and the page fetches the latest forecast each time you open it or choose a new place.

Forecasts are estimates and can change, especially for showers and thunderstorms more than a day ahead. For severe weather warnings, follow your national weather service.

Your location and settings

"Use my location" only runs when you choose it, and your browser asks for permission first. The site remembers your last place and your choice of °C or °F on your own device. The privacy policy explains this and how ads on the site work.

Why there are ads

Hourly Forecast is free to use. Ads pay for the weather data and hosting, and every ad is labelled "Advertisement" so it's clear what is forecast and what isn't.
