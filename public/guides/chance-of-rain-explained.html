What “chance of rain” really means

Updated 26 November 2026

A 40% chance of rain is one of the most misunderstood numbers in weather. It doesn't mean it will rain for 40% of the hour, or over 40% of the area. Here's what it does mean, and how to use it.

The short answer

The chance of rain is the probability that at least a small amount of rain, drizzle or snow falls at a particular place during a particular period. On Hourly Forecast that period is one hour.

A 40% chance means that, in weather situations like this one, rain falls at that spot about four times out of ten. Six times out of ten, it stays dry.

What it doesn't tell you

The percentage says nothing about how heavy the rain will be or how long it will last. A 90% chance of light drizzle and a 30% chance of a heavy thunderstorm are both possible on the same day. To get the full picture, read the percentage together with the conditions column, which shows the expected type of weather for that hour, such as light rain, showers or thunderstorm.

Where the number comes from

Weather models simulate the atmosphere forward in time. Because small differences in today's conditions can grow into big differences later, forecasters run the same model many times with slightly different starting conditions. This set of runs is called an ensemble.

If most runs produce rain at your location in a given hour, the chance is high. If only a few do, it's low. Hourly Forecast uses precipitation probability from Open-Meteo, which is built from ensemble forecasts and counts an hour as wet if at least 0.1 mm of rain or melted snow falls, which is a very light amount.

Rules of thumb

ChanceWhat it usually means
Under 20%Rain is unlikely. Most people won't need an umbrella.
20% to 50%Rain is possible. Take a light jacket or umbrella if you'll be out for a while.
50% to 70%Rain is more likely than not. Plan outdoor activities around it.
Over 70%Expect rain. Check the hours either side for a drier window.

These are guides, not rules. If getting wet would ruin your plans, treat even a moderate chance seriously.

Why showers are tricky

On days with scattered showers, rain falls in small patches that drift with the wind. One street can get soaked while the next stays dry. Forecasts on these days often show a moderate chance spread over many hours, because the models agree showers will form but can't say exactly where. A long run of 30% to 50% hours usually means "on and off", not "steady rain".

Rain or snow?

The same percentage covers rain, drizzle, sleet and snow. Look at the conditions for that hour, and at the temperature: near or below freezing, precipitation is more likely to fall as snow.
