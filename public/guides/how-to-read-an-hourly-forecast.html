How to read an hourly weather forecast

Updated 26 November 2026

An hourly forecast shows what the weather is likely to do hour by hour, which makes it far more useful than a daily summary when you're planning a walk, a commute or an event. Here's what each part means and how to get the most from it.

Start with the timing, not the icon

A daily forecast that says "rain" could mean a ten-minute shower at 6 am or a wet afternoon. The hourly view tells you which. On Hourly Forecast, the sentence at the top of the page looks at the next 24 hours and tells you when rain or snow is likely, based on the hours with a 50% chance or higher. If no hour reaches that, it tells you whether it should stay dry or whether there's a slight chance of showers.

Use that sentence to decide when to go out, then check the hours either side in the table for the details.

Times are local to the place you're viewing

Every time on the forecast is the local time of the place you're looking at, not the time on your device. If you check the forecast for Tokyo from London, "3 pm" means 3 pm in Tokyo. The current local time is shown next to the sunrise and sunset times.

Temperature and feels like

Temperature is the air temperature two metres above the ground, the standard height weather stations use. "Feels like" adjusts it for wind, humidity and sunshine to estimate how the air will feel on your skin. On a windy winter day the two can be several degrees apart, so dress for the feels-like number. Our guide to feels-like temperature explains how it's worked out.

Chance of rain or snow

The percentage is how likely it is that at least a small amount of rain or snow falls at that place during that hour. It doesn't say how heavy the rain will be or how long it will last. A 30% chance means rain is possible but less likely than not. Read more in what "chance of rain" really means.

Wind

Wind speed is the steady wind at ten metres above the ground. Gusts can be noticeably stronger, especially in hilly or built-up areas. The letters show the direction the wind is coming from, and the arrow points the way it's blowing. See wind speed and direction for what different speeds feel like.

Humidity and UV index

Humidity is the relative humidity for each hour. High humidity on a warm day makes it feel sticky; on a cold day it feels damp. The UV index shows how strong the sun's ultraviolet radiation is. It's zero at night and usually peaks around the middle of the day. Our guides to humidity and the UV index go into more detail.

How far ahead to trust it

Forecasts are most reliable for the next 12 to 24 hours and become less certain further out. Temperature trends tend to hold up well over a few days. The exact timing and position of showers and thunderstorms is much harder to predict, because they form quickly and can be only a few kilometres across.

For anything that depends on the weather, check the forecast again closer to the time. The forecast on this site updates every hour, so a fresh look on the morning of your plans gives you the latest picture.

A quick routine

  • Read the outlook sentence to see whether and when rain is expected.
  • Scroll the 48-hour strip to see how the temperature rises and falls.
  • Check feels-like and wind for the hours you'll be outside.
  • In summer, look at the UV index around midday.
